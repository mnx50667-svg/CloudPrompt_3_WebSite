How to Sync Your AI Prompts Across Devices with Google Drive

By CloudPrompt Team · Updated October 2026

You write a prompt on your work laptop that finally gets the summary format right. That evening, at your home desktop, you need it and it is not there. You either retype it from memory or dig through a chat history that lives in a different browser profile. If you use AI tools on more than one computer, keeping your prompts in step is a real, daily friction. This guide looks at the common ways people move prompts between machines, what to look for in a sync setup that respects your data, and how CloudPrompt approaches it using your own Google Drive.

How people usually move prompts between machines

Most of us start with whatever is at hand. Each of these works, up to a point.

Emailing or messaging yourself. Fast and familiar. The downside is that your prompts are now scattered across dozens of messages with no structure, and finding "that one about meeting notes" means searching your inbox. Updates create new messages rather than replacing the old one, so you end up with several versions and no clear current one.

A notes app. Better, because notes are organized and searchable. The problems appear at scale: one long note becomes a wall of text, notes apps are not built for quickly inserting text into a web page, and if the app is tied to one ecosystem, your other computer may not have it.

A text file in a cloud folder. Simple, portable and under your control. It works well for a handful of prompts. Past that, you are scrolling a long file, editing in two places risks conflicts, and there is no quick way to pull a prompt into the browser without switching windows.

Relying on chat history. The assistant remembers your conversations, so why not just scroll back? Because history is per tool, often per account, and organized by time rather than by task. It also stores the whole exchange rather than the reusable prompt.

Browser sync of bookmarks or tabs. Some people save prompts as bookmark names or keep tabs open. This is fragile, hard to search, and breaks the moment you clear a session.

None of these are wrong. They just stop scaling once you have more than a dozen prompts you care about.

What to look for in a sync setup

If you are going to rely on something to keep prompts in step across devices, it is worth being deliberate about what you want from it.

  • Your own storage. Prefer a setup where the data sits in an account you already control, rather than on a third party's server. If the tool disappears, your prompts should still be yours.
  • Narrow access. If a tool connects to your cloud storage, it should only be able to touch the files it creates, not everything else in there.
  • No extra account. Every new account is another password and another place your data lives. Fewer is better.
  • Automatic background sync. You should not have to remember to upload or download anything. Change a prompt on one machine, see it on the other.
  • Backups before risky changes. Imports, bulk edits and merges are where things go wrong. A copy taken right before helps.
  • Export in a plain format. JSON or CSV you can open in a text editor or spreadsheet. This is your exit route if you ever change tools.
  • Offline access. You should still be able to read your prompts on a plane or a flaky connection.
  • Visible status. Some signal that tells you whether you are signed in and whether changes have synced, so you are not guessing.

You can meet most of these with a careful manual setup. The question is whether you want to maintain it.

Sync, backups and export do different jobs

Sync keeps the same library on every computer. A backup is a copy taken before a risky change, so you can step back if something goes wrong. An export is a separate file you keep or move to another tool. A good setup gives you all three, because sync copies mistakes to every device just as faithfully as it copies improvements.

The manual approach with Google Drive

If you prefer to do it yourself, a reasonable baseline is a single plain-text or JSON file in a Google Drive folder, opened from each computer. Keep one prompt per block with a short title line, and edit in one place at a time to avoid conflicts. Export is trivial because the file already is the export. Backups are up to you: copy the file before a big reorganization.

What this approach lacks is the last mile. The prompts are synced, but getting one into a chat window still means switching to the file, finding the prompt, copying, switching back and pasting. For a few prompts a day that is fine. For twenty, it adds up.

How CloudPrompt syncs with Google Drive

CloudPrompt is a free browser extension for Chrome, Edge and Comet that takes the manual approach above and removes the friction, while keeping your data where you can see it.

Your prompts are stored in your own Google Drive, in a CloudPrompt folder, as a file called prompts.json. There is no CloudPrompt account to create, and nothing is stored on CloudPrompt servers. The file is yours, in your Drive, in a readable format.

Access is narrow. CloudPrompt uses Google's drive.file permission, which means it can only access files it creates. It cannot see the rest of your Drive.

Syncing across computers is a matter of installing CloudPrompt on each one and connecting the same Google account. Each installation then sees the same library, and changes sync automatically in the background. You connect once with Google's secure sign-in (OAuth). If your sign-in ever expires, your prompts stay on screen and CloudPrompt asks you to reconnect.

Before changes that could risk data loss, CloudPrompt makes an automatic backup to your Drive. You can also import and export your whole library as JSON or CSV at any time, so you have a plain copy independent of the extension.

When you are offline, prompts cached on your device remain available. The toolbar icon shows sync status, including whether you need to sign in or whether changes are waiting to sync, and you get storage usage alerts.

If you would like your prompts to follow you between your work and home computers without an extra account to manage, install CloudPrompt from the Chrome Web Store and connect your Google account on each machine.
